1. Problem Description¶

A strip footing of width B = 2 m carries a centric vertical load and lies at the ground surface (Df = 0).
The soil parameters are:

  • Cohesion (c = 15) kPa
  • Internal friction angle (φ = 30^\circ)
  • Unit weight (γ = 20) kN/m³

The ultimate bearing capacity is calculated using Terzaghi's bearing capacity equation:

[ q_u = cN_c + \gamma D_f N_q + 0.5 B \gamma N_\gamma ]

Since (D_f = 0):

[ q_u = cN_c + 0.5 B \gamma N_\gamma ]

Statistical Model Specification¶

The soil parameters (c, φ, γ) are assumed to follow lognormal distributions.
The coefficient of variation (CoV) for each parameter is assumed to be 0.10.

In [4]:
# Lognormal Parameter Conversion

# Mean values of soil parameters
mean_c = 15
mean_gamma = 20

# Friction angle converted to tan(phi)
mean_tanphi = np.tan(np.radians(30))

# Coefficient of variation (given in assignment)
cov = 0.10


# Function to convert mean and CoV into lognormal parameters (mu, sigma)
def lognormal_params(mean, cov):
    
    # Standard deviation in log-space
    sigma = np.sqrt(np.log(1 + cov**2))
    
    # Mean in log-space
    mu = np.log(mean) - sigma**2 / 2
    
    return mu, sigma


# Compute lognormal parameters for each soil property
mu_c, sigma_c = lognormal_params(mean_c, cov)
mu_gamma, sigma_gamma = lognormal_params(mean_gamma, cov)
mu_tanphi, sigma_tanphi = lognormal_params(mean_tanphi, cov)

Monte Carlo Simulation¶

Monte Carlo simulation is performed with 500 realizations of the soil parameters to estimate the probabilistic distribution of ultimate bearing capacity.

In [6]:
# Number of Monte Carlo simulations
N = 500

# Randomly generate soil parameters from their lognormal distributions
c = np.random.lognormal(mu_c, sigma_c, N)
gamma = np.random.lognormal(mu_gamma, sigma_gamma, N)
tanphi = np.random.lognormal(mu_tanphi, sigma_tanphi, N)

# Convert tan(phi) back to friction angle phi
phi = np.arctan(tanphi)

# Footing width
B = 2

# Empty list to store computed bearing capacities
qu = []

# Loop through all samples to compute ultimate bearing capacity
for i in range(N):

    # Calculate bearing capacity factors for each phi
    Nq = np.exp(np.pi*np.tan(phi[i]))*(np.tan(np.radians(45)+phi[i]/2)**2)
    Nc = (Nq - 1)/np.tan(phi[i])
    Ngamma = 2*(Nq + 1)*np.tan(phi[i])

    # Terzaghi bearing capacity formula
    qu_i = c[i]*Nc + 0.5*B*gamma[i]*Ngamma

    # Store result
    qu.append(qu_i)

# Convert list to NumPy array
qu = np.array(qu)

Sensitivity/Importance Analysis¶

Using the trained ANN model, a parameter sensitivity analysis was performed to determine the relative importance of the input parameters (cohesion c, friction angle φ, and unit weight γ) on the predicted ultimate bearing capacity qu.

The results show that the friction angle (φ) has the strongest influence on the ultimate bearing capacity. This is because the bearing capacity factors Nq and Nγ increase exponentially with φ. Therefore, even small variations in the friction angle can produce large changes in the predicted bearing capacity.

Cohesion (c) has a moderate influence because it contributes linearly to the bearing capacity through the term cNc in Terzaghi’s equation.

The unit weight (γ) has the least influence among the three parameters because it appears only in the third term of the equation and its effect on the final bearing capacity is relatively smaller.

Therefore, the relative importance of the parameters can be summarized as:

φ > c > γ

This indicates that uncertainty in the friction angle contributes the most to the variability of the ultimate bearing capacity.

Reporting and Conclusions¶

Summary of Findings

The deterministic bearing capacity calculated using the mean soil parameters was approximately 900 kPa.

A probabilistic analysis was performed using Monte Carlo simulation with 500 realizations of soil parameters to account for uncertainty in soil properties. The statistical results obtained from the simulation are approximately:

Mean ultimate bearing capacity ≈ 928 kPa Standard deviation ≈ 288 kPa Coefficient of variation ≈ 0.31 5th percentile ≈ 556 kPa 95th percentile ≈ 1488 kPa

These results indicate that the ultimate bearing capacity varies significantly due to uncertainty in soil parameters.

The ANN surrogate model was trained to predict the ultimate bearing capacity using soil parameters as inputs. The model achieved an R² value of approximately 0.997, indicating that the ANN accurately approximates the relationship between soil parameters and bearing capacity.

The sensitivity analysis shows that the friction angle (φ) is the most influential parameter controlling the uncertainty in the predicted bearing capacity.

Suggestions to Reduce Uncertainty

To reduce uncertainty in foundation design, more accurate characterization of the friction angle should be performed. This can be achieved by conducting additional laboratory tests such as direct shear tests or triaxial tests. Improving the accuracy of friction angle measurements will significantly reduce uncertainty in predicting the ultimate bearing capacity and lead to safer and more reliable foundation designs.
